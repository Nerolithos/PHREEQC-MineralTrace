## Why the charge errors occur

The synthetic total recipe is nominally CaF2 + CaCl2 + NaHCO3. It is not an actual preparation or measurement. Prescribing pH independently of those totals changes bicarbonate/carbonate/CO2, proton/hydroxide distributions and complexation. Temperature also changes speciation. Therefore a nominal recipe does not remain electrically neutral over an arbitrary T–pH scan. Those points are unbalanced initial-speciation scenarios; they are not failed PHREEQC solves.

Element totals cannot simply be assigned fixed ionic charges: total inorganic carbon includes differently charged aqueous species. `charge_equivalents` instead sums `molality × ionic charge` for explicit aqueous species: cation and anion equivalents in eq/kgw, net equivalents and `100*(cation-anion)/(cation+anion)`. A zero denominator remains unknown. No total-carbon = carbonate shortcut is used.

A second diagnostic parses PHREEQC's rounded species distribution and sums ionic equivalents independently of SELECTED_OUTPUT. The v0.2.0 42-point comparison differs from engine percent errors by at most about 0.00331 percentage points, consistent with printed-species rounding; it is not an independent activity calculation. For exact classification, the engine's high-precision percent error is used. Real-engine balanced and unbalanced synthetic CaF2 + NaCl fixtures test warning behavior without any charge adjustment.

## Separate statuses and policies

| Dimension | States | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Computation | success / failed, plus failure_kind | Process/output completeness; distinguishes known missing phase and identified numerical failure from generic engine/output failure |
| Chemical quality | pass / fail / unverified | Agreement with the **declared diagnostic charge threshold**; not proof of valid measurements or thermodynamics |
| Model applicability | verified / invalid / unverified | Missing phase is invalid for the requested calculation. Current full Ca–F–C model remains unverified even when scalar checks pass |
| Observation constraints | pass / fail / unverified | User constraints, stage scope and provenance policy; no independent authentication of the user's data |
| Final inclusion | included / included_conditional / excluded / unverified | Conjunction under the selected policy; SI status is retained separately |

Strict policy excludes hard failures and leaves unknown applicability, unknown evidence or missing sources unverified. There is currently no fully verified model profile, so strict final inclusion is zero: this is an honest validation boundary, not evidence that no geological environment exists.

Exploratory policy may retain a chemistry-pass, SI-compatible, evidence-compatible candidate **conditionally** despite unverified applicability or a missing source. It still excludes charge-threshold failures and does not include unevaluated constraints. Missing source identifiers are not invented. Both tracks remain explicit in reports.

The 0.1% default is the existing diagnostic threshold, not a universal sample-quality limit. Users can explicitly declare another policy in `[evaluation]` or a quality constraint. Original adapter warnings at 0.1% are retained even when a different evaluation threshold is requested; changing a policy never erases the warning record.

## Region and contribution semantics

Raw SI, standalone chemical admissibility, observationally constrained SI/chemistry candidates, final inclusion and failed/unverified masks remain distinct. Warning masks and exclusion masks are exported separately. Observation-constrained regions are vacuous with respect to observations when none were supplied; this is not positive observational evidence.

Contribution reports retain two tracks: SI-and-chemistry candidates before the model gate, and candidates eligible under the final policy. Sequential filtering follows declaration order; leave-one-out retains all other constraints. Empty physical baselines do not establish evidence conflict; absent or unevaluable evidence does not establish contradiction. Redundancy and conflicts describe the sampled grid only, never universal logical redundancy or physical probability.

No electroneutrality-adjustment scenario is implemented. Unknown adjustment options are rejected, no measured concentration is altered, and every original PHREEQC deck is preserved.
